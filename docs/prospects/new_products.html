🛣️ Cold asphalt (bagged cold-mix) 20

📊 Opportunity: MARGINAL / CONDITIONAL-GO. Workable as a niche branded-retail + municipal supply play, but NOT a strong commodity-arbitrage. Source from Iran if pursued; use UAE only as a re-export/paperwork layer; benchmark Turkey as the real competitor before committing.
HS code2715 (2715.00.00) — "bituminous mixtures based on natural/petroleum bitumen; cut-backs, mastics." Sits in HS Chapter 27 (mineral/bituminous products), which is the correct code for bagged cold-mix asphalt / cold patch.
Priced perTwo units in the market: (1) RETAIL — 25 kg multi-wall kraft/PP bag on pallets (~40 bags = 1 metric ton); (2) WHOLESALE/EXPORT — priced per metric ton (MT). Jumbo bags (500–1000 kg) and bulk loose load also offered. For sourcing math, per-MT is the working unit; per-25kg-bag is the retail unit.
Iran EXWESTIMATE ~$130–170/MT (≈ $3.25–4.25 per 25 kg bag). No published cold-asphalt EXW quote was found (ATDM lists the product but quotes only on request). Derived from Iran bitumen FOB ~$343/MT jumbo-bag (Jan 2026) at ~5–6% binder + cheap aggregate + bagging/palletizing (~$40–80/MT) + margin. Bulk (unbagged) cold mix would be lower, ~$60–100/MT.
UAE EXWESTIMATE ~$200–250/MT (≈ $5.00–6.25 per 25 kg bag). No firm published quote; derived from higher UAE bitumen/aggregate input, labor and overhead, and UAE's quality/re-export positioning. ~30–45% above Iran EXW.
Georgia duty0% (high-confidence ESTIMATE — HS 2715 is a Chapter 27 mineral/bituminous product in Georgia's 0% duty band; ~90% of Georgian tariff lines are 0% and 2715 is not a domestically-protected construction material). Residual risk of 5% only if a customs officer reclassifies it as a "construction material" — confirm on the Georgian Revenue Service tariff calculator before committing volume.
Georgia VAT18% (confirmed standard rate; charged on CIF+duty, but recoverable/creditable for a VAT-registered importer reselling domestically, so it is a cash-flow item rather than a true margin cost for a trader).
Delivered to Georgia (est.)ALL ESTIMATES. Iran via truck (through Armenia/Azerbaijan to Tbilisi): CIF ~$250–290/MT; landed ~$270–290/MT VAT-exclusive (real cost basis for a VAT-registered reseller) or ~$320–350/MT incl. 18% VAT ≈ $6.8–8.7 per 25 kg bag. UAE via sea to Poti: landed ~$370–390/MT VAT-exclusive (~$435–460/MT VAT-inclusive) ≈ $9.3–11.5 per bag. Duty 0%; VAT 18% recoverable; broker/clearance ~$5–10/MT.
Cheapest sourceIran — cheaper than UAE at both EXW (~$130–170 vs ~$200–250/MT) and delivered-Tbilisi (~$280–350 vs ~$370–460/MT VAT-in). Iran wins on the world's cheapest bitumen plus a shorter overland route. (Caveat: Turkey, not analyzed as a source here, is the realistic delivered-cost competitor and may beat Iran.)
BenchmarkItalian/European bagged cold-mix asphalt — "Kaltasphalt Plus" class (also ASPHALTALL, Kaugummiasphalt, EZ Street, UPM, McAsphalt Perma-Patch, already used in Tbilisi). — European retail €15–20 per 25 kg standard (~$16–22/bag; ~$640–880/MT); premium polymer-modified €35.90/25 kg (~$1,400/MT). US EZ Street/UPM $10–20 per 50 lb bag. In Georgia, imported branded cold patch would retail higher after freight+margin — ESTIMATE ~$12–25+ per 25 kg bag at retail and ~$450–800/MT at regional wholesale/landed (premium brands more).
MARGIN: On paper the spread is attractive — Iran landed Tbilisi ~$280–350/MT (VAT-in) vs European benchmark wholesale ~$450–800/MT and retail $640–1,400/MT, implying a healthy gross umbrella if you can sell at benchmark-adjacent prices. DEMAND: Real and recurring — Georgia's road network needs pothole/winter patching (municipalities + DIY retail); imported cold patch (McAsphalt) is already used in Tbilisi. LOGISTICS is the core weakness: this is a low value-to-weight product (bagged rocks + a little bitumen), so freight is 30–45% of landed cost and eats most of the arbitrage — the cheap-bitumen edge shrinks dramatically once you truck/ship it. COMPETITION: Turkey is the structural threat — it borders Georgia, has an FTA (0% duty anyway on Ch.27), cheap trucking and clean banking, and can likely match or beat Iran delivered; local hot-mix plants also compete for bulk municipal work, leaving cold patch a niche. UAE is not cost-competitive (higher EXW + longer sea leg). RISK: Iran adds payment/sanctions/banking friction (hard USD/LC settlement) and reputational/origin issues — the main reason to route through a UAE re-export entity. NET: Iran is genuinely the cheapest source and the nominal margin is real, but freight economics, a stronger Turkish competitor, and Iran payment friction make this an opportunistic, brand/service-differentiated niche (premium bagged cold patch for Georgian retail + municipal contracts) rather than a scalable price-arbitrage line. Recommend: (1) get firm EXW cold-asphalt quotes from 2–3 Iranian producers and a Turkish producer; (2) confirm HS 2715 = 0% on the GE Revenue Service calculator; (3) pilot one truckload before scaling. All prices marked ESTIMATE are derived, not firm quotes.

🟩 Rubber tiles (gym / outdoor) 24

📊 Opportunity: Qualified YES — a good product to source into Georgia, but the opportunity is specifically the IRAN-as-producer lane; UAE->Georgia does NOT work on economics and is only useful as a sanctions-clean re-export conduit at thinner margin.
HS code4016.91 (full line 4016.91.0000) — "floor coverings and mats, of vulcanized rubber other than hard rubber." Correct classification for SBR/EPDM gym and outdoor sports rubber tiles.
Priced perSquare meter (m2) is the standard B2B pricing and sales unit. Tiles are physically supplied as 500x500mm (4 tiles = 1 m2) or 1x1m pieces; bulk lots are sometimes quoted per metric ton. Physical spec that drives logistics: a 20mm gym tile weighs ~16-20 kg/m2 (density ~1,000-1,300 kg/m3), so this is heavy, low value-density cargo.
Iran EXW~$4-8/m2 EXW, midpoint ~$6/m2 — ESTIMATE (no firm factory quote obtained; direct RFQ required). Applies to standard 10-20mm SBR / recycled tire-crumb gym tiles. Iran is a genuine low-cost producer (cheap energy, domestic tire-crumb feedstock, low labor). Higher-spec / EPDM export quotes ran up to ~$14-17/m2. Sold per m2, priced by thickness, finish and volume.
UAE EXW~$9-16/m2 EXW, midpoint ~$12/m2 — ESTIMATE (no firm quote; RFQ required). The UAE is a re-export / distribution hub rather than a low-cost manufacturer: "made-in-UAE" tile carries higher overhead, and much stock is re-exported Chinese product with a markup. UAE installed/retail prices run AED 120-350/m2 (~$33-95/m2), far above EXW, so retail is not a proxy for factory cost.
Georgia duty0% most likely — ESTIMATE, verify the exact 4016.91 line with the Georgian Revenue Service. Georgia uses three MFN bands (0% / 5% / 12%); ~90% of goods are 0%, and the 5%/12% bands target domestically-produced agricultural, food and construction/textile goods. Vulcanized-rubber articles (Chapter 40) are industrial imports Georgia does not protect, so 0% is the strong default. IMPORTANT: Iran and the UAE have no FTA with Georgia, so they pay the MFN rate (China and Turkey would be 0% by FTA regardless).
Georgia VAT18% standard VAT, levied on the CIF customs value plus any import duty. Recoverable by a VAT-registered importer that resells domestically, so for a trading business it is a cash-flow item, not a true cost.
Delivered to Georgia (est.)All figures ESTIMATE (per m2, standard 20mm SBR gym tile). IRAN route ~$9-12/m2 landed Tbilisi incl. VAT: EXW ~$6 + overland truck via Armenia/Azerbaijan ~$2-3.5 + clearance/insurance ~$0.5 = CIF ~$7-11; 0% duty (est.); +18% VAT ~$1.3-2 (VAT recoverable if importer is VAT-registered and reselling, so effective goods cost ~$7-11). UAE route ~$14-20/m2 landed incl. VAT: higher EXW ~$12 + Jebel Ali->Poti sea freight (~$1,253/20ft container ≈ under $1/m2) + inland/clearance ~$0.5 = CIF ~$12-16; 0% duty (est.); +18% VAT ~$2-3. For reference, the Chinese benchmark delivered lands ~$12-19/m2 (0% duty under China-Georgia FTA).
Cheapest sourceIran — clearly cheapest, roughly 30-45% below UAE delivered (Iran ~$9-12/m2 vs UAE ~$14-20/m2 landed Tbilisi, estimates). The reason is structural: Iran actually manufactures the tile cheaply and is close enough to ship overland, whereas the UAE only re-exports (largely Chinese) product at a markup and adds no production cost advantage.
BenchmarkChinese SBR/EPDM gym rubber tiles — Qingdao SOL Industrial (SOL Rubber) and Wonder Rubber & Plastic Co. These are the price/quality reference and enter Georgia DUTY-FREE (0%) under the China-Georgia FTA, unlike Iran/UAE which pay MFN. — SOL Rubber EXW China (per m2): entry 8mm interlock $4-8; mid-spec 20mm compound rubber $9.60-13.75; 50mm interlock ~$12.20-12.80; premium EPDM $21-25. Overall benchmark band ~$6-14/m2 EXW, with the most common gym-grade SBR tile ~$8-12/m2. MOQ ~100-520 m2. Regional wholesale/retail (installed) sits much higher (UAE ~$33-95/m2 retail).
MARGIN: Iran lands at ~$9-12/m2 incl. VAT (effective goods cost ~$7-11/m2 after VAT recovery). Against a plausible Georgian wholesale/resale of ~$15-30/m2 (estimate), that is a ~40-100%+ gross margin — attractive for a heavy building-material SKU. UAE at ~$14-20/m2 landed leaves little room versus the same resale prices and versus duty-free Chinese product, so UAE is margin-thin. DEMAND: Georgia's fitness-center, sports-facility, playground and construction sectors are growing (Tbilisi/Batumi development, sports tourism), so demand is real — but the country is small (~3.7M people), so treat this as a modest-volume niche or one line within a broader building-materials import book, not a standalone business. LOGISTICS: The tile is heavy and low value-density (16-20 kg/m2), so freight is a large share of landed cost and cargo is weight-limited (a 20ft box or a truck both max out near ~1,200-1,550 m2). This structurally rewards the nearest low-cost producer — Iran overland — over both China and UAE by sea. Caveat: Iran and Georgia share no border, so goods transit Armenia or Azerbaijan, adding a border/transit-risk layer. COMPETITION: The benchmark is Chinese (SOL/Wonder) product that enters Georgia at 0% duty under the China-Georgia FTA, is brand-consistent and well-financed at $6-14/m2 EXW. Iranian tile must beat delivered Chinese price AND match acceptable quality; its proximity gives the delivered-cost edge, but Chinese quality/brand/credit terms are the real competitor. UAE product is just arbitraged China, so it can never win on price. GATING RISKS: (1) Iran sanctions and banking — USD payment, LCs and bank transfers to Iran are the single biggest execution and legal/reputational risk; deals often route through third-country intermediaries (frequently the UAE), barter or cash, which must be solved before scaling. (2) Quality consistency of Iranian recycled-SBR tile (odor, cure, dimensional tolerance) — sample and QC before committing. (3) Confirm the 4016.91 duty line is truly 0% (a 12% surprise would compress margin). (4) Small absolute market. BOTTOM LINE: Strong economics on Iran->Georgia (real producer + proximity + heavy-cargo freight logic + likely 0% duty + recoverable VAT); weak on UAE->Georgia. Proceed on the Iran lane only if the payment/sanctions channel and quality QC are solved; otherwise use the UAE purely as a clean re-export channel accepting lower margin.

🏃 Pour-in-place rubber flooring 22

📊 Opportunity: CAUTIOUSLY POSITIVE / niche-viable as a TURNKEY INSTALLATION business — NOT as a raw-granule reseller. Selling imported granules alone barely beats easy-to-buy Chinese material on landed cost, so margins there are thin. The real margin is in installed turnkey surfacing: landed material runs ~$30-45/m2 while imported EPDM wet-pour installs regionally for ~$45-90/m2, i.e. 40-60% gross before labor. Georgian demand is real and government-driven (kindergartens, school running tracks, municipal playgrounds, gyms — recurring public tenders), though the total market is small (pop ~3.7M). Georgia's 0% duty + narrow 18%-of-CIF VAT base and free-trade posture make it an unusually friendly import jurisdiction. Recommend pursuing: Iran-first sourcing of SBR + PU binder (biggest weight and cost), paired with a quality colored EPDM top (Iran or China), competing on installed price and local presence — with UAE as the banking/logistics fallback. Do a paid sample order and one pilot install before scaling.
HS codeTwo-part system, two HS chapters: (1) Rubber granules — synthetic EPDM/SBR under HS 4002 (synthetic rubber, primary/granule form) or HS 4004.00 (rubber granulate/scrap-derived crumb); (2) Polyurethane binder — HS 3909 (amino/PU resins in primary form; some 1-K MDI binders classify under 3506/3909). Finished pre-made rubber tiles would instead fall under 4008/4016, but the wet-pour KIT (loose granules + liquid binder) travels as 4002/4004 + 3909.
Priced perTraded/priced PER METRIC TON for each input (colored EPDM granules, SBR crumb, PU binder), shipped as a kit. The finished installed surface is quoted PER SQUARE METRE (m2) at a stated thickness. Both units are given below.
Iran EXWESTIMATE (no firm quote obtained; Iranian factories quote on request, often rial/barter). Colored EPDM granules ~$1,300-1,900/MT; SBR crumb ~$200-350/MT; PU binder ~$1,600-2,200/MT. Iran is a domestic petrochemical producer (SBR from Bandar Imam/Takht-e-Jamshid; MDI/polyols from Karun) so SBR and binder are structurally cheap; weak rial deepens the discount. EPDM colored-granule processing is less mature than China, so quality/color consistency is the main variable. Per finished 40mm playground system: ~$22-32/m2 material EXW (EST).
UAE EXWESTIMATE. Colored EPDM granules ~$1,800-2,600/MT; SBR crumb ~$350-550/MT; PU binder ~$2,200-2,900/MT. Note: the UAE is essentially a re-export/trading hub — only one genuine EPDM granule maker exists (Elji International, Dubai); most "UAE suppliers" import Chinese/European material and add a 10-25% trader margin. So UAE EXW roughly equals China FOB plus a middleman markup, with no real raw-cost advantage. Per finished 40mm system: ~$30-42/m2 material EXW (EST).
Georgia duty0% — raw synthetic rubber/granules (Ch. 40: 4002/4004) and PU resin binder (Ch. 39: 3909) are non-domestically-produced industrial inputs and sit in Georgia's 0% band (which covers ~90% of tariff lines); the 5% and 12% bands apply only to goods Georgia produces (certain agri, food, construction materials). No FTA required to reach 0%.
Georgia VAT18%, levied on (CIF value + duty); since duty is 0%, VAT is effectively 18% of the CIF value. Plus a nominal customs clearance fee (~5 GEL if value <3,000 GEL, ~60 GEL/~$22 above that). VAT is recoverable if the importer is VAT-registered and on-sells/installs domestically.
Delivered to Georgia (est.)ESTIMATE, landed Tbilisi/Poti = EXW + freight + 0% duty + 18% VAT (+ ~5-60 GEL clearance). FROM IRAN (land via Azerbaijan/Armenia or Bandar Abbas->Poti, freight ~$150-250/MT): EPDM ~$1,750-2,450/MT, SBR ~$300-500/MT, binder ~$2,100-2,850/MT; finished 40mm system ~$30-42/m2 landed material. FROM UAE (Jebel Ali->Poti, freight ~$200-350/MT): EPDM ~$2,350-3,300/MT, binder ~$2,900-3,700/MT; finished 40mm system ~$40-55/m2 landed material. Duty is 0% for both origins (no Georgia FTA needed since the band is already 0%); VAT 18% applies to the CIF value.
Cheapest sourceIRAN is cheaper on every input — roughly 20-40% below UAE landed, driven by domestic SBR/PU petrochemical feedstock, subsidized energy, a depreciated rial, and a shorter overland route to Georgia. BUT the saving carries friction: sanctions/banking (no clean LC or USD wires — cash, barter or third-country routing), variable EPDM color consistency, and documentation/reputational risk. UAE costs 20-40% more but delivers clean USD/LC banking, reliable certificates of origin, consistent (Chinese/European-sourced) quality, and Jebel Ali logistics. Practical play: source the heavy, cheap, low-risk inputs (SBR base + PU binder) from Iran, and use UAE as a clean-banking/re-invoicing intermediary or for the quality-critical colored EPDM top layer when Iranian direct payment is blocked.
BenchmarkChinese EPDM wet-pour system (colored EPDM granules + 1-K PU binder kit) is the volume price-setter; premium benchmark is European EPDM (German Melos/Conica, Italian Mondo). China FOB material ~$20-30/m2 for a 40mm system; European runs 2-3x. — China FOB granules+binder ~$20-30/m2 material (40mm system) -> ~$35-55/m2 landed Georgia; regional installed turnkey EPDM playground/track ~$45-90/m2 installed (ESTIMATE); US reference $8-20/sqft ($86-215/m2) installed, labor-inflated. Component FOB China: colored EPDM $1,500-2,800/MT, SBR $230-550/MT, PU binder $1,500-2,800/MT.
MARGIN: raw inputs are cheap (SBR ~$0.25-0.50/kg, EPDM ~$1.3-2.0/kg, binder ~$1.6-2.2/kg landed); value-add is coloring, binder chemistry, logistics and on-site installation skill. Reselling raw granules only undercuts Chinese landed material by ~10-25% (thin, contestable). Turnkey installation converts $30-45/m2 landed material into $45-90/m2 installed revenue = healthy gross. DEMAND: steady, publicly funded (schools, kindergartens, stadiums, playgrounds) but modest in absolute size; lumpy tender-driven cash flow. LOGISTICS: Iran holds a genuine proximity/cost edge (overland + Bandar Abbas->Poti) offset by sanctions/banking friction; UAE is frictionless but offers no raw-cost edge; Georgia itself is 0-duty, low-VAT-base and trade-friendly. COMPETITION: Chinese kits dominate on price and buy-ability (LC-ready), European brands (German Melos/Conica, Italian Mondo) own premium/spec tenders; to win, differentiate on installed turnkey price + local presence, not on selling loose granules. KEY RISKS: Iranian EPDM color/quality consistency, Iran payment/sanctions routing, and small market depth — mitigate with a pilot order and by anchoring the business on installation contracts rather than commodity trading. All Iran/UAE figures are ESTIMATES; obtain firm factory quotes before committing.
