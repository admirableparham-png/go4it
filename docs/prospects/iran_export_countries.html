Zinc sulphate

9 countries

Honest framing: Iran's real strength is upstream - it is a regional zinc-metal titan (Angouran/Mehdiabad mines, Zanjan smelting hub, 320k+ t/yr lead-zinc), which gives it CHEAP feedstock for zinc sulphate; but finished-ZnSO4 export is still nascent and one Iranian source flags these specialty compounds as 'difficult to export.' So the winning plays are NOT the biggest global importers (US, EU, China, India) - those are either sanctions-closed or self-sufficient net exporters - but Iran's NEIGHBOURS that combine (a) genuine agronomic zinc deficiency and (b) a land/barter lane that dodges Western banking. Tier 1 (strong): Pakistan (top-3 global ZnSO4 buyer + severe Punjab/Sindh zinc deficiency + border barter trade, no banking needed), Iraq (77% of soils zinc-deficient, Iran already the dominant supplier), and Afghanistan (Iran = 30% of its imports, chemical imports +51% in 2024, small but locked-in). Tier 2 (moderate): Russia (top-3 importer, INSTC corridor + ruble/rial settlement make it the most navigable LARGE market, but Iran competes on freight not price), Bangladesh (top-3 global ZnSO4 destination and an unsubsidized open private-import market, but a pure price/sea play via UAE), Turkey (better as a feedstock/re-export partner than an end-market), and the UAE (not real end-demand but the essential Jebel Ali re-export + payment gateway to Africa/South Asia). Tier 3 (watch): Turkmenistan and Azerbaijan - low-volume but low-friction neighbour beachheads via the INSTC. Where demand is thin, it concentrates in the cotton/wheat belts reachable overland. Do NOT chase the US, EU/UK, Canada/Australia (sanctions-closed) or China/India (net exporters). Evidence: Volza/Eximpedia trade data, USDA/FAS Bangladesh fertilizer report, Iraqi soil-zinc study, Iran-Pakistan border/barter measures (2024-26), INSTC 2024 volumes, and the Sept-Oct 2025 EU/UN snapback sanctions.

Why they import One of the top three global destinations for zinc-sulphate monohydrate (Volza). Severe, structural agronomic need: calcareous Punjab/Sindh soils at pH 8+ lock up zinc, and ZnSO4 is the front-line defense against 'Khaira' disease in the rice belt and a standard input for wheat/cotton. Large, recurring seasonal fertilizer demand, not a one-off.
Iran can win because This is Iran's single best fit: shared land border and CHEAP zinc feedstock (Angouran/Zanjan) beat freight-in Chinese/Indian product. 2025+ signal: 2025-26 border build-out: Taftan formalized as a land customs station (FBR SRO1055 of 2026), Kohak-Cheedgi crossing opened Jan 2025, Gabd-Rimdan Dec 2024; both governments targeting $10B bilateral trade by 2030 (from ~$3B in 2025).

Copper sulphate

9 countries

Global copper-sulphate (HS 283325) trade was ~$863M in 2024, up 6% YoY, but the three biggest buyers - Belgium ($184M), the US ($103M) and Australia ($46.3M) - are exactly the markets that are closed to Iranian origin (OFAC/EU/Australian sanctions, euro-USD clearing loss, REACH/vendor registration). Iran is also NOT a global cost-leader in copper sulphate specifically: the top exporters are South Africa, Taiwan and Mexico, and Iran's own copper-sulphate production is still nascent (small lines, 'first line being installed'), even though Iran is a giant copper miner with cheap smelter feedstock, sulphuric acid and energy. So the real, winnable opportunity is regional: neighbors on existing land lanes where Iran competes on price + proximity and can settle in barter/rial rather than dollars. Best bets in order: Iraq (Iran's #1 copper market ~29%, booming date/palm agriculture, live oil-for-goods barter), Pakistan (24/7 borders, $10B trade target, authorized barter, huge cotton/rice/citrus fungicide demand), and Afghanistan (Iran's #2 copper market ~17%, grape/orchard fungicide need, Iranian goods already dominant - excellent lane but small, unstable). Second tier: Turkey (real viticulture demand but domestic producers and banking scrutiny cap the win) and the UAE (best as a Jebel Ali re-export/payment gateway rather than an end market). Watch list: Turkmenistan and Uzbekistan (Central Asian cotton agriculture reachable only via the Iran/Trans-Caspian corridor), India (strong and growing ~14-17 kt demand but domestic supply plus cautious Iran-exposed banks), and Brazil (enormous demand but no Iran lane - big market, wrong supplier). Concentrate effort on Iraq, Pakistan and Afghanistan first.

Saffron

10 countries

Global saffron imports are only ~$243M/yr (2024, down ~12% over 5 yrs), and Iran supplies the bulk of it but almost entirely as unbranded bulk that intermediaries repackage. ~82% of Iran's official saffron exports land in just four countries — UAE, China, Spain, Afghanistan — all of which act as re-export/rebrand hubs rather than final consumers. For an Iran-origin export broker, real opportunity = markets where Iranian-origin can physically land AND get paid: Gulf hubs with hawala/exchange-house settlement (UAE, Qatar, Kuwait), non-dollar lanes (China via yuan/CIPS + Hong Kong side-door), neighbour land-borders (Afghanistan, Turkey), and the EU-legal bulk repackagers (Spain). Big obvious markets (US, Canada, UK, Australia, Israel) are effectively CLOSED to direct Iranian-origin and only receive it after Afghan/UAE/Spanish re-labeling — don't chase them directly. Iran's edge everywhere is price (bulk fell from $3,000/kg in 2011 to under $900 in 2023), proximity, and quality reputation of sargol/pushal; its handicap is banking friction and tariff disadvantage vs zero-tariff Afghan-origin in some markets (notably India).

Pistachios

9 countries

Rank the book by where Iran already wins on price, proximity, an existing lane, or a working non-dollar payment rail - not by raw market size, because the biggest markets (US, EU, UK, Canada) are exactly the ones closed to Iranian origin by sanctions/banking or fresh food-safety bans. China is the top prize: world's #1 importer, Iran's fastest-growing destination, cheapest-supplier economics, and yuan settlement that defeats dollar sanctions - reachable directly or laundered via Turkey/Vietnam re-export. UAE is both a strong buyer (Ramadan demand) and the payment/logistics gateway (dirham, Dubai front companies, 26% of Iran's sales re-export through it). India, Turkey, Russia, Iraq round out the strong tier: all combine real demand with proximity/corridor and dollar-free settlement (rupee-rial, lira/FTZ barter, the EAEU FTA + INSTC for Russia/Kazakhstan, land-border cash for Iraq). Honest caveats: Iran's 2025 crop was drought-hit and total exports slipped ~11% YoY, and the US is aggressively undercutting Iran on premium grades in India and via Vietnam - so compete on price, in-shell/kernel snack grades, and the markets where getting PAID is solved.
